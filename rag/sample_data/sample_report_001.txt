CYBERSECURITY AUDIT REPORT
Report ID: AR-2026-001
Auditee: Example Financial Services Pvt. Ltd.
Audit Period: 01 Jan 2026 - 31 Jan 2026
Audit Standard Reference: ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0

Executive Summary

This report presents the findings of a cybersecurity audit conducted on the
IT infrastructure and information security management system of Example
Financial Services Pvt. Ltd. The audit assessed access control, patch
management, incident response, logging and monitoring, and third-party
risk management practices. Overall, the organisation demonstrates a
moderately mature security posture with several high-priority gaps
requiring remediation within 30 days.

Scope of Audit

The audit covered all production servers, the corporate network perimeter,
the customer-facing web application, and the organisation's incident
response procedures. Cloud infrastructure hosted on the organisation's
primary IaaS provider was included. Third-party vendor systems were
excluded except where data-sharing agreements were in scope.

Methodology

The audit team conducted interviews with IT and security personnel,
reviewed policy documentation, performed configuration reviews of a
sample of 25 servers, and analysed six months of security event logs.
Vulnerability scanning was performed using an authenticated scan against
all in-scope systems between 05 and 09 January 2026.

Findings

Finding 1 - Multi-Factor Authentication Coverage
Multi-factor authentication (MFA) is enforced for all administrative
accounts accessing production systems via the central identity provider.
However, three legacy service accounts identified during the configuration
review were found to authenticate using password-only access. These
accounts have not been remediated as of the report date.

Finding 2 - Patch Management
The organisation maintains a documented patch management policy requiring
critical security patches to be applied within 14 days of release. Review
of patch logs for the audit period showed that 92% of critical patches
were applied within the required window. Eight servers were found to be
running an operating system version that reached end-of-life support six
months prior to the audit.

Finding 3 - Logging and Monitoring
Centralised logging is implemented via a SIEM platform that ingests logs
from network devices, servers, and the identity provider. Log retention is
configured for 180 days, meeting the organisation's internal policy
requirement of 90 days minimum. Alerting rules exist for failed login
thresholds, but no alerting rule currently exists for anomalous outbound
data transfer volumes.

Finding 4 - Incident Response
An incident response plan exists and was last reviewed 14 months ago,
exceeding the organisation's internal annual review requirement. The plan
defines roles, escalation paths, and communication procedures. No
tabletop exercise or simulated incident drill was conducted during the
preceding 12 months.

Finding 5 - Vulnerability Management
The vulnerability scan identified 14 medium-severity and 2 high-severity
vulnerabilities. Both high-severity vulnerabilities relate to an outdated
version of a web server component on the customer-facing application and
have an available vendor patch. As of the report date, one of the two
high-severity vulnerabilities remains unpatched, 11 days after
identification, against an internal SLA of 7 days for high-severity
findings.

Finding 6 - Data Encryption
Data at rest in the primary production database is encrypted using
AES-256. Data in transit between the web application and the database is
encrypted via TLS 1.2. Backup media stored at the organisation's offsite
facility is not encrypted.

Finding 7 - Third-Party Risk Management
The organisation maintains a vendor risk register covering 18 active
third-party data-processing relationships. Annual security questionnaires
were completed for 15 of the 18 vendors during the audit period. Contracts
for the remaining three vendors do not contain a data breach notification
clause.

Recommendations

R1. Remediate the three password-only legacy service accounts identified
in Finding 1 by enforcing MFA or decommissioning the accounts, within 30
days.

R2. Upgrade or replace the eight end-of-life servers identified in Finding
2, and review the patch SLA against the 8% of critical patches that missed
the 14-day window.

R3. Implement an alerting rule for anomalous outbound data transfer
volumes to close the gap identified in Finding 3.

R4. Conduct an annual incident response tabletop exercise, with the next
exercise scheduled within 90 days.

R5. Patch the remaining high-severity vulnerability identified in Finding
5 immediately; the current delay already exceeds the internal 7-day SLA.

R6. Extend encryption-at-rest coverage to offsite backup media identified
in Finding 6.

R7. Obtain updated third-party contracts including a data breach
notification clause for the three vendors identified in Finding 7.

Conclusion

Example Financial Services Pvt. Ltd. demonstrates a generally sound
security control environment with documented policies covering the core
domains assessed. The audit identified one overdue high-severity
vulnerability and several process gaps that should be prioritised for
remediation. No critical, unremediated findings from the prior audit
cycle were identified as still open.
